READING THE RATINGS

A common scale.
A personal challenge.

Ratings are individual AI assessments of the ideas, prerequisites and proof required. They are approximate, not official contest ratings or measured solve probabilities.

1–3.5Easier than the preferred range
4Lower edge of harder BMO1
5–6Core harder BMO1 territory
7Upper edge · accessible BMO2
7.5–10Harder than the preferred range

Half-point steps allow a little more detail. The bank includes every source-verified, individually assessed question regardless of rating. Your preferred range of 4–7 is the default filter; choose “All 1–10” to browse easier and harder questions too. Question number and competition name alone never set a rating.

What “individually assessed” means

Each published problem has been read and assessed from its mathematical content, using a sourced solution or independent solution analysis. The hidden rating note explains the main difficulty. An elegant short proof can still involve a demanding insight.

Solutions and spoilers

Solutions, detailed technique tags and rating explanations stay hidden until you reveal them. Each question also has consistent subtopics, such as Polynomials, Pigeonhole principle or Invariants. These appear on cards and can be filtered; untick “Show subtopics on cards” to hide them while browsing. Some entries link to a published worked solution; others contain an original exposition of the mathematics. The source and type are identified on each entry.

Coverage and provenance

This is a reviewed collection, not a claim that every historical olympiad has been exhausted. The Coverage page distinguishes papers actually screened from archives still to review. Translations and uncertain provenance are noted on the relevant problem.
